Chapter 01

The light that stayed

2 minute read

Reading settings

The last lamp on the street should have gone out at midnight.

Mara knew this because she had put it out herself. She had climbed the iron ladder, turned the little brass key, and watched the borrowed memory inside the glass fold into darkness. She had done the same thing every night for eleven years.

And yet, when she reached the corner, her shadow was still in front of her.

She stopped. A cart rattled somewhere beyond the canal. Rain had begun to gather on the shoulders of her coat, the patient sort of rain that never seemed to arrive and somehow left you soaked.

Behind her, the lamp was burning blue.

“Not tonight,” she said.

The lamp did not appear to consider this a reasonable request.

Mara went back. The ladder was wet now, and the iron cold enough to make her fingers ache. At the top, she pressed her face close to the glass.

Ordinary memories burned gold. Happy ones were bright and unreliable; sad ones lasted longer. Everyone in the city knew that. It was why the richest streets were not always the brightest, and why the lamps outside the hospital never needed tending.

Blue was something else.

Inside the flame, a woman stood on a bridge. She had Mara’s coat and Mara’s tired hands. In the distance, the city was utterly dark.

Then the woman turned.

Do not come home, she said.

Mara let go of the ladder.

The pavement met her sooner than she expected. For a while, she could do nothing but sit in the rain and consider whether eleven years of good service entitled her to leave a lamp burning.

A door opened across the street.

“You saw it, then,” said a small voice.

In the doorway stood a boy carrying an unlit candle. He looked about nine, though the way he waited made Mara think of old clocks.

“What is that light?” she asked.

The boy looked up at the lamp.

“Tomorrow,” he said. “And we haven’t much time to change it.”
